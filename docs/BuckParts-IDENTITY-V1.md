## 5. Twenty-year sequence (identities over time)

**Source:** Phase 8 §5  
**Claim label in source:** INFERRED

- **Act 1 (years 0–3) — Consumer trust brand:** “fit lookup for your home” + don't-buy honesty + the fit-guarantee essence; earn households one burning need (filters) at a time; email-first; rails activated; the negative dataset and demand telemetry accrue as by-products.
- **Act 2 (years 2–7) — The household equipment registry:** “My Home Filters” grows into “My Home” — the system-of-record for the home's equipment, earned through Act 1's use case (the only path that dodges the Centriq cold-start grave). Replenishment revenue matures (affiliate → direct → subscription); switching costs and first-party data become the real moat.
- **Act 3 (years 5–20, conditional) — Fitment-truth infrastructure:** licensed to distributors, marketplaces, and AI assistants — exercised ONLY on buyer evidence, quite possibly realized as the acquisition exit rather than an operated business.

**Act transition rule:** Act 2 is reachable only through Act 1's replenishment use case. Act 3 is conditional and evidence-gated. Registry-first and infrastructure-first are rejected as starting identities.

**Domain / proving-ground note:** Identity locks the act sequence (filters → earned registry → conditional infrastructure). New problem domains are not implied by Identity alone; Constitution §8 (one proving ground; explicit charter) still governs. Detailed adjacency maps belong to Strategy Doctrine when authored — not here.

## 6. Rejected identities (nine-candidate disproof)

**Source:** Phase 8 §4  
**Claim label in source:** INFERRED (disproof analyses); starting position PROVEN

| Identity | Identity ruling |
|---|---|
| **Decision Engine** | Rejected as terminal company identity — engines become features of whoever owns the customer |
| **Truth Operating System** | Rejected as company identity — OS remains the internal engine |
| **Infrastructure / Data Company** (fitment-truth API) | Rejected as **starting** identity; preserved as Act 3 optionality / most probable strategic exit |
| **Consumer Brand (household replenishment)** | Survives as **Act 1**; pure content brand alone plateaus without the earned registry |
| **Marketplace** | Rejected — capital and competition |
| **Enterprise Software** | Rejected — no motion, no category |
| **AI Platform** | Rejected as identity; may appear only as positioning inside the infrastructure option |
| **Household Equipment Registry (“My Home”)** | Survives as **Act 2** only — never registry-first (Centriq precedent) |
| **Fit-Risk Underwriter** | Rejected as full identity; **wins as brand essence** of the consumer act |

### Why every other identity loses (preserved)

- **Decision Engine** loses because engines end up as features of whoever owns the customer — this identity owns the customer instead.
- **Truth OS / Enterprise Software** lose because nobody buys epistemology; the OS remains the factory, not the product.
- **Infrastructure/Data first** loses on founder fit, dataset scale, and capital — but is deliberately preserved as Act 3 optionality and the most probable exit; starting there means enterprise sales with nothing to sell.
- **Marketplace** loses on capital, inventory, and a head-on war with incentive-aligned incumbents who would otherwise be customers or acquirers.
- **AI Platform** loses because it names the tool, not the trust; BuckParts feeds AI agents truth — it does not compete to be one.
- **Registry-first** loses to the Centriq precedent — registries are earned through a burning need, never adopted cold; the recommendation sequences it as Act 2 rather than discarding it.
- **Underwriter-as-identity** loses on balance-sheet shape but survives as the single most differentiated promise the brand can make.
- **Pure consumer content brand** — the closest loser — loses only because it plateaus without the registry's compounding switching costs; the recommendation is that brand PLUS the registry it earns.
