## 4. Identity ranking and rationale (disproof-first)

### IDENTITY-BY-IDENTITY DISPROOF

| Identity | Disproof-first analysis | $100M / $1B | Why it fails (or survives) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision Engine** | A product, not a company. Decision utilities get absorbed as features (every checker/calculator in history). No standalone revenue model; founder fit fine but ceiling is a feature-acquisition | <3% / ~0 | Terminal identity fails: engines become features of whoever owns the customer |
| **Truth Operating System** | No buyer category exists — companies buy outcomes, not epistemology. Enterprise dev-tools sale with a solo founder; the methodology is copyable; the OS's value is what it PRODUCES | <2% / ~0 | Loses on buyer reality and founder fit; the OS stays the internal engine |
| **Infrastructure / Data Company** (fitment-truth API) | Phase 7 stands: dataset 4 orders below relevance, enterprise sales motion absent, agent-era timing UNKNOWN, $1-3M+ to attempt. BUT: the asset appreciates as a by-product of consumer work, distributors are incentive-aligned buyers, and the same asset is the defense AND the exit | ~10-15% IF a distributor-class customer lands / <2% | Loses as STARTING identity (capital + fit); survives as harvested optionality and the most probable strategic exit |
| **Consumer Brand (household replenishment)** | Best founder fit; bootstrappable; AI-search kills traffic-acquisition (mitigated by email-first) and AI assistants may absorb replenishment interfaces long-term (mitigated: assistants need fit-truth — the registry can plug INTO them); distributor attack per Phase 7 (defensible via independence, breadth, speed) | ~5-10% alone / ~0 | Survives as ACT ONE — but as a *pure* content brand it plateaus; needs the registry to compound |
| **Marketplace** | Inventory, fulfillment, working capital, head-on war with distributors. Solo-impossible | ~0 / ~0 | Loses instantly on capital and competition |
| **Enterprise Software** | Same buyer-reality and founder-fit failures as Truth OS | <2% / ~0 | Loses — no motion, no category |
| **AI Platform** | BuckParts is a truth SOURCE for AI, not an AI platform. Identity-as-buzzword | — | Loses as identity; wins as positioning inside the infrastructure option |
| **Discovered: Household Equipment Registry (“My Home” as system-of-record)** | The genuinely bigger idea — the home's equipment inventory + parts/manuals/recalls/maintenance downstream. DISPROOF: registry-FIRST is a proven graveyard (Centriq — the precedent this repo's own fork test named as the trap). Registries aren't adopted; they're EARNED as by-products of one burning repeated need | Lifts the composite to ~8-12% / ~1% | Survives as ACT TWO — reachable only through the replenishment use case |
| **Discovered: Fit-Risk Underwriter** (“we guarantee it fits”) | Converting truth into guarantee economics is the sharpest brand essence available — nobody else can offer it credibly. As a full identity it becomes insurance-shaped balance-sheet risk | — | Loses as identity; **wins as the brand essence and killer feature** of the consumer act (“fit guaranteed or we make it right”) |

### Why every other identity loses

- **Decision Engine** loses because engines end up as features of whoever owns the customer — this identity owns the customer instead.
- **Truth OS / Enterprise Software** lose because nobody buys epistemology; the OS remains the factory, not the product.
- **Infrastructure/Data first** loses on founder fit, dataset scale, and capital — but is deliberately preserved as Act 3 optionality and the most probable exit; starting there means enterprise sales with nothing to sell.
- **Marketplace** loses on capital, inventory, and a head-on war with incentive-aligned incumbents who would otherwise be customers or acquirers.
- **AI Platform** loses because it names the tool, not the trust; BuckParts feeds AI agents truth — it does not compete to be one.
- **Registry-first** loses to the Centriq precedent — registries are earned through a burning need, never adopted cold; the recommendation sequences it as Act 2 rather than discarding it.
- **Underwriter-as-identity** loses on balance-sheet shape but survives as the single most differentiated promise the brand can make.
- **Pure consumer content brand** — the closest loser — loses only because it plateaus without the registry's compounding switching costs; the recommendation is that brand PLUS the registry it earns.

## 5. Twenty-year sequence

**The twenty-year sequence (identities over time, explicitly):**
- **Act 1 (years 0–3) — Consumer trust brand:** “fit lookup for your home” + don't-buy honesty + the fit-guarantee essence; earn households one burning need (filters) at a time; email-first; rails activated; the negative dataset and demand telemetry accrue as by-products.
- **Act 2 (years 2–7) — The household equipment registry:** “My Home Filters” grows into “My Home” — the system-of-record for the home's equipment, earned through Act 1's use case (the only path that dodges the Centriq cold-start grave). Replenishment revenue matures (affiliate → direct → subscription); switching costs and first-party data become the real moat.
- **Act 3 (years 5–20, conditional) — Fitment-truth infrastructure:** licensed to distributors, marketplaces, and AI assistants — exercised ONLY on buyer evidence, quite possibly realized as the acquisition exit rather than an operated business.
